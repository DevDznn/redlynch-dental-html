Full arch dental implantsA fixed set of teeth, supported by implants

If you wear a full denture that moves, or most of your teeth are failing, full arch dental implants may be an option. A small number of implants in the jaw support a complete set of fixed teeth that are not taken out at night. Redlynch Dental assesses suitability, plans the treatment with a 3D scan and explains the full cost before anything goes ahead.

Who asks about full arch implants

  • People whose full denture slips when they eat or talk, or who use adhesive every day.
  • People who have lost most of the teeth in one jaw, or whose remaining teeth are loose, decayed or breaking.
  • People told they need all their teeth removed and want a fixed alternative to a conventional denture.
  • People with an existing implant-retained denture who want teeth that stay in.

What full arch implants are

A full arch replaces all the teeth in the upper or lower jaw on a single fixed bridge. The bridge is supported by four to six dental implants, small titanium posts placed in the jawbone. Once the implants have healed and bonded with the bone, the bridge is secured to them. It doesn’t come out at night and doesn’t need adhesive. It is cleaned in the mouth and reviewed by your dentist like natural teeth.

How treatment works

  1. Consultation and 3D scan

    Your dentist examines your mouth, takes a 3D X-ray (CBCT) and digital impressions, and talks through your health, your goals and your options. You’ll be told at this stage whether full arch implants suit you.

  2. Planning

    The implant positions and the shape of the new teeth are planned from the scan before any treatment starts. You receive a written treatment plan with the full cost and the sequence of appointments.

  3. Implant placement

    The implants are placed in the jawbone under local anaesthetic. Any remaining teeth in that arch are removed at the same appointment if they haven’t been already.

  4. Healing

    The implants bond with the bone over several months. The timeframe differs between patients and your dentist will give you yours.

  5. Final teeth

    Once healed, the final bridge is made to match your face, bite and preferences, then fitted and adjusted.

  6. Reviews

    Regular check-ups to check the implants, the gums and the bridge, and to clean areas you can’t reach at home.

Is it right for you?

Not everyone is suitable. Your dentist will look at the amount and quality of bone in your jaw, the health of your gums, your general health and any medications, and whether you smoke. Some people need extra procedures first, such as bone grafting.

If a full arch isn’t the right option, the alternatives are an implant-retained denture (a removable denture that clips onto two or more implants), a conventional denture, or individual implants and bridges where enough natural teeth remain. Your dentist will go through all of these with you, including what happens if you choose to do nothing for now.

Things to consider

  • It involves surgery and a healing period of months, with some swelling and discomfort in the first days.
  • Treatment runs across several appointments, usually over three to six months or longer depending on healing.
  • The bridge and implants need daily cleaning and regular professional maintenance. Gum disease around implants can lead to implant loss.
  • As with any surgery there are possible complications, including infection, nerve disturbance, implants that don’t bond with the bone, and a bridge that needs repair or replacement over time. Your dentist will discuss the risks that apply to you.
  • Smoking, uncontrolled diabetes and some medications affect healing and long-term success.

Cost and payment

The cost of a full arch depends on the number of implants, whether teeth need to be removed, whether grafting is needed, and the type of bridge. Because of that, Redlynch Dental doesn’t quote a single figure before seeing you. After your consultation and scan you receive a written plan with the total cost and what each stage covers.

If you have extras cover, your health fund may contribute towards part of the treatment. Payment plans are available. See payment and costs for more.

Questions

Questions patients ask

Still unsure? Call (07) 4039 4720 and talk to our team.

How long does the whole process take?

Usually three to six months from the first consultation to the final bridge, longer if grafting is needed. Your dentist will give you a timeframe for your own case.

Will I be without teeth?

What you have in place while the implants heal depends on your case. Your dentist will explain what applies to you before treatment starts.

Is it painful?

Implants are placed under local anaesthetic so you are numb during the procedure. Some swelling, bruising and discomfort is normal for a few days afterwards and is managed with the pain relief your dentist recommends.

Am I too old for implants?

Age on its own isn’t the deciding factor. Bone, gum health and general health are. Your consultation and 3D scan will show whether implants suit you.

How do I clean a full arch bridge?

Brushing, cleaning under the bridge with the tools your dentist shows you, and regular professional cleans. It’s cleaned in the mouth, not taken out.

Does my health fund cover it?

Extras cover may contribute towards some items. What you get back depends on your policy, so check with your fund using the item numbers in your treatment plan.
