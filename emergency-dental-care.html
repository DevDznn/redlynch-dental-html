What counts as a dental emergency

If it hurts, it’s broken or it’s swollen, call us.

Dental emergencies are often serious, and if left untreated they can get worse quickly. A dental emergency may include:

Painful tooth or gums

Throbbing, constant or night-time pain, or pain when biting, can be a sign of infection or deep decay.

Right nowTake pain relief as directed and avoid very hot, cold or sweet foods. Call us.

Broken teeth and fillings

Even without pain, a broken tooth or lost filling can crack further, trap food or expose the nerve.

Right nowKeep any broken pieces, rinse gently, and avoid chewing on that side.

Facial swelling

Swelling of the face, jaw or gums can mean an infection that needs prompt attention.

Right nowCall us straight away. If swelling affects breathing, swallowing or your eye, call 000.

Trauma to the face, teeth or gums

A knock or fall that chips, loosens, moves or knocks out a tooth, or injures the gums or lips.

Right nowKnocked-out tooth? Hold it by the crown, keep it moist in milk, and call immediately.

Broken or lost denture

A cracked, broken or lost denture can make eating and speaking difficult.

Right nowKeep all the pieces and don’t try to glue it yourself. Call us.

Not sure if it’s an emergency?

Call anyway. Tell us what has happened and we will advise you on what to do next.

What happens next

What to do, step by step

  1. Call us straight away

    Phone (07) 4039 4720. Tell us what happened, where it hurts, how long it has been going on and whether there is any swelling.

  2. We find you a time

    Specific appointments are reserved every day for emergencies. We will offer the earliest suitable time and tell you what to do in the meantime.

  3. Assessment and relief

    The dentist examines the problem, takes any X-rays that help, and focuses on relieving your pain and stabilising the problem.

  4. A plan for what’s next

    If you need further treatment, we explain your options and costs before booking anything.

Questions

Emergency dental questions

When you call, our team will advise you based on your situation.

Can I be seen today?

We reserve specific appointments every day for dental emergencies, and will always strive to make time to see you as soon as possible. Call as early as you can. Same-day appointments depend on availability, so call us straight away.

What if it’s outside opening hours?

Our hours are Monday and Tuesday 8am to 5:30pm, Wednesday and Thursday 8am to 6:30pm, and Friday 8am to 4:30pm. We are closed on weekends. Outside these hours, if your symptoms are severe or getting worse, go to your nearest hospital emergency department or call 000. For health advice, you can call 13 HEALTH (13 43 25 84) in Queensland.

Should I go to hospital instead?

Go to a hospital emergency department or call 000 if you have swelling affecting your breathing, swallowing or eye; bleeding that won’t stop; a serious head or facial injury; or if you feel very unwell with a high fever. For most other dental pain, broken teeth and lost fillings, call us.

What can I do for a toothache until I’m seen?

Take over-the-counter pain relief as directed on the packet or by your pharmacist, rinse gently with warm water, and avoid very hot, cold or sweet foods. Don’t put aspirin or other tablets directly on the gum. Call us to arrange an appointment.

A tooth has been knocked out. What do I do?

Call us immediately: time matters. Handle the tooth by the crown (the white part), not the root. If it is dirty, rinse it briefly in milk. Keep it moist in milk, or for adults, inside the cheek, and bring it with you. Don’t try to put a baby tooth back in. We will give you more instructions on the phone.

Do you see new patients for emergencies?

Yes. Redlynch Dental welcomes new patients of all ages, including for emergencies. Call us to arrange an appointment.
