Oral surgery

Calm, careful and at your pace.

From the Redlynch Dental team

We understand that having a tooth removed can be a serious source of anxiety. Our team is highly trained both in removing teeth as comfortably as possible and in making sure you are as relaxed as possible during the process.

If you need a tooth removed, we will take the time to discuss any reservations you have, and what additional steps we can take so we overcome them together.

Why a tooth may need to come out

Severe decay or damage

When a tooth is too broken down to be repaired with a filling, crown or root canal therapy.

Infection

An infected tooth that can’t be saved, or where saving it isn’t the right choice for you.

Advanced gum disease

When the bone supporting a tooth has been lost and the tooth is loose.

Wisdom teeth and crowding

Impacted or problem wisdom teeth, or sometimes to make space for orthodontic treatment.

Saving the tooth comes first. Where it is possible and sensible, your dentist will talk through options for keeping a tooth, such as a filling, crown or root canal therapy, so you can make an informed choice.

What to expect

  1. Before

    We examine the tooth, take an X-ray, go through your medical history and medications, and explain the procedure and your options.

  2. On the day

    The area is numbed with local anaesthetic. You may feel pressure, but you shouldn’t feel sharp pain. Tell us at any time if you need a break.

  3. Afterwards

    We give you written aftercare instructions and talk about options for replacing the tooth if needed.

Aftercare: the first few days

  • Bite gently on gauze to help a blood clot form, and rest for the remainder of the day.
  • Avoid rinsing, spitting forcefully, drinking through a straw, smoking and alcohol for the period your dentist advises. These can disturb the clot.
  • Eat soft foods and chew on the other side.
  • Take pain relief as directed by your dentist or pharmacist.

Call us if pain gets worse a few days after the extraction (this can be a sign of dry socket), if bleeding won’t stop with firm pressure, or if you notice increasing swelling or a fever. If swelling affects your breathing or swallowing, call 000.

Any surgical or invasive procedure carries risks. Before proceeding, you should seek a second opinion from an appropriately qualified health practitioner.

Questions

Extractions: your questions answered

Still unsure? Call (07) 4039 4720 and talk to our team.

Will it hurt?

The tooth and surrounding area are numbed with local anaesthetic, so you shouldn’t feel sharp pain during the extraction. Some discomfort afterwards is normal, and we will explain how to manage it.

I’m very anxious. What can be done?

Tell us when you book. We take time to discuss your concerns, explain each step, and can agree a signal for you to pause. For some procedures, such as wisdom tooth removal, a sedative can be prescribed to take beforehand where appropriate.

Should I replace the tooth?

It depends on where the tooth is and how it affects your bite and appearance. Options include an implant, a bridge or a denture.
