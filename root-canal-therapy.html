Saving natural teeth

Root canal therapy

When the nerve inside a tooth is inflamed beyond repair or has died, root canal therapy can save the tooth and avoid the need to remove it.

Book online (opens the practice's online booking system in a new tab) (07) 4039 4720
From the Redlynch Dental team

Root canal therapy is needed when the nerve in your tooth is inflamed beyond repair or has died. This can happen because of deep decay or an injury or knock to the tooth. During treatment, the nerve is removed from the tooth and replaced with a filling material.

Despite what you may have heard, the majority of root canal treatments are completed comfortably under local anaesthetic. Root canal treatment can be a safe and long-lasting option for patients who want to save their natural teeth and avoid having them removed.

Signs you may need a root canal

  • Lingering pain, especially when biting or chewing
  • Sensitivity to heat or cold that lasts after the trigger is gone
  • Swelling or tenderness in the gum near a tooth, or a pimple on the gum
  • A tooth that has darkened after a knock

Sometimes there are no symptoms at all, and the problem is found on an X-ray.

What happens during treatment

  1. Access

    The tooth is numbed and a small opening is made to reach the canals inside the root.

  2. Cleaning

    The inflamed or infected nerve tissue is removed, and the canals are cleaned and shaped.

  3. Sealing

    The canals are filled and sealed, and the tooth is closed with a filling.

  4. Protecting

    A crown is often recommended afterwards, especially for back teeth, to protect the tooth from breaking.

Treatment often takes more than one appointment. Your dentist will explain the plan for your tooth.

Swelling or severe pain? A tooth infection can spread. Call us promptly, and see emergency dental care. If swelling affects your breathing or swallowing, call 000.

Any surgical or invasive procedure carries risks. Before proceeding, you should seek a second opinion from an appropriately qualified health practitioner.

Questions

Root Canal Therapy: your questions answered

Still unsure? Call (07) 4039 4720 and talk to our team.

Is a root canal painful?

Most root canal treatment is carried out comfortably under local anaesthetic. The tooth may feel tender for a few days afterwards, and we will advise you on pain relief.

Why not just take the tooth out?

Keeping your natural tooth helps you chew properly and stops neighbouring teeth from drifting. A missing tooth may also need replacing later with an implant, bridge or denture. Your dentist will explain the pros and cons of each option.

Will I need a crown afterwards?

Often, particularly for back teeth, because root-filled teeth can become more brittle. See crowns and bridges.
