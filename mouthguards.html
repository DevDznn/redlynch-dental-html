Sport and protection

Mouthguards

A properly fitted mouthguard can greatly reduce the risk of injury to your teeth and jaws during contact sport. People of all ages should wear one.

Book online (opens the practice's online booking system in a new tab) (07) 4039 4720
From the Redlynch Dental team

A mouthguard is a protective device worn on your upper teeth during contact sport, which can greatly reduce or eliminate injury to your teeth and jaws. People of all ages should wear a mouthguard, including children, as injuries can be devastating to their developing teeth.

What a mouthguard helps protect against

Damaged teeth

Chipped, broken or knocked-out teeth.

Jaw injuries

Jaw fractures and damage to the jaw joints.

Soft tissue

Cuts to the lips and cheeks.

The practice also lists concussion among the injuries a mouthguard can help reduce. Head injuries can still happen with a mouthguard, so follow your sport’s concussion guidelines.

Custom-fitted vs over-the-counter

Custom-fitted (from your dentist)Over-the-counter
FitMade from a mould of your teethGeneric sizing, or shaped at home
ComfortDesigned to stay in place so you can breathe and speak more easilyCan be bulky and may loosen during play
ProtectionA mouthguard custom-made by a dentist provides the most protection for the teeth and mouth.

Caring for your mouthguard

  • Rinse it after use and clean it gently with a soft toothbrush.
  • Store it in a ventilated case and keep it out of hot cars and direct sun, which can warp it.
  • Bring it to your check-ups so we can check the fit. Children’s mouthguards need replacing as their teeth and jaws grow.

Questions

Mouthguards: your questions answered

Still unsure? Call (07) 4039 4720 and talk to our team.

Which sports need a mouthguard?

Any sport with a risk of contact with a ball, another player or the ground, such as football codes, hockey, basketball, netball, cricket, martial arts and skateboarding.

How often should my child’s mouthguard be replaced?

Children’s mouths change as teeth come through and jaws grow, so their mouthguard should be checked regularly and replaced when it no longer fits well.

What if a tooth is knocked out during sport?

Call us straight away. See emergency dental care for what to do.
