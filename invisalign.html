Teeth straightening

Clear aligners

A series of clear, removable trays that gradually move your teeth into position. In suitable cases, clear aligners can achieve results similar to traditional braces.

Book online (opens the practice's online booking system in a new tab) (07) 4039 4720
From the Redlynch Dental team

Clear aligner treatment straightens and aligns your teeth, often giving a result similar to traditional metal braces. A series of clear plastic trays is fitted to your teeth and changed every two weeks, with each tray moving your teeth closer to their final position.

Advantages of clear aligners

Discreet

Virtually invisible compared with traditional metal braces.

Removable

Taken out for up to two hours a day, so you can eat your normal foods and brush and floss as usual.

No wires or brackets

Nothing metal to poke or irritate your cheeks and gums.

Fits your lifestyle

Comfortable, with minimal impact on your day-to-day routine.

How it works

  1. Assessment

    A detailed examination with one of our dentists to check suitability and talk through your goals, time frames and costs.

  2. Records

    X-rays, photos and moulds of your teeth are sent to the aligner laboratory to plan your treatment.

  3. Aligners

    You usually start wearing your trays within a few weeks, changing to the next set every two weeks.

  4. Retention

    Once treatment is complete, retainers hold your teeth in their new position.

Results depend on wearing your aligners. Aligners only move teeth while they are in your mouth, so they need to be worn for most of the day and night, as your dentist instructs.

Questions

Clear Aligners: your questions answered

Still unsure? Call (07) 4039 4720 and talk to our team.

Can I have clear aligners?

Many people are good candidates, but clear aligners don’t suit everyone. Your dentist will assess your individual needs. In some cases you may need traditional braces or a referral to a specialist orthodontist.

How long does clear aligner treatment take?

It depends on your needs. Some minor cases can take as little as seven weeks, but average treatment time is usually around 12 to 18 months.

What happens when I finish?

You need to commit to keeping your new smile. This can mean clear retainers, a wire bonded to the inside of your teeth, or both. Without retention, teeth tend to move back towards where they started.

How do I get started?

Your first visit is a detailed examination to check suitability and discuss time frames and costs. From there we take X-rays, photos and moulds to send to the aligner laboratory.
