Dentures

Tired of a rocking denture? Need dentures for the first time? Or want implants to anchor your denture in place? We can help with all of these.

From the Redlynch Dental team

Tired of your old rocking dentures? Do you need dentures for the first time? Maybe you want some implants to anchor down your denture? Whatever your question, our team is trained to provide you with the very best in tooth replacement solutions.

Types of dentures

Full dentures

Replace all the teeth in the upper or lower jaw, resting on the gums.

Partial dentures

Replace one or several missing teeth, clipping onto your remaining natural teeth.

Implant-retained

Dentures that click onto dental implants for more stability when you eat and speak.

Signs your denture needs attention

  • It rocks, slips or feels loose when you eat or talk.
  • Sore spots or ulcers on your gums that don’t settle.
  • It is cracked, chipped or a tooth has come off.
  • It no longer looks or feels the way it used to. Gums and bone change shape over time, so dentures that once fitted well may not any more.

Broken or lost denture? This is one of the dental emergencies we reserve appointments for. See emergency dental care or call (07) 4039 4720.

Caring for your dentures

  • Clean your dentures every day with a soft brush, and rinse them after meals.
  • Take them out at night, unless your dentist advises otherwise, and store them as recommended.
  • Handle them over a folded towel or a basin of water in case you drop them.
  • Keep having regular check-ups. Your dentist will check your gums, the fit of your denture, and the soft tissues of your mouth.

Questions

Dentures: your questions answered

Still unsure? Call (07) 4039 4720 and talk to our team.

How long does it take to get new dentures?

It depends on the type of denture and whether any teeth need to be removed first. Making a denture normally takes several appointments for impressions, fittings and adjustments. We will give you a timeline at your consultation.

Can implants stop my denture moving?

In many cases, yes. Implants can be used to anchor a denture so it is more stable. Suitability depends on your bone and general health, which we assess with an X-ray. See dental implants.

Will it feel strange at first?

New dentures often feel unusual for the first few weeks while you get used to eating and speaking with them. Sore spots can usually be eased with small adjustments, so come back and see us.
